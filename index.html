Why founders don't claim

Three reasons come up again and again in practitioner guidance.

"That's for big companies"Most dollars still go to large manufacturers. The startup payroll-tax route is far less known.
"The paperwork will kill us"The four-part test, contemporaneous records and audit risk scare off small teams.
"Where would I even look?"The IRS, 50 state revenue departments, SBIR agencies and universities each publish separately.

How the model works, and what it would take to do better
  1. Eligible pool: Census County Business Patterns 2022 establishments in R&D services (5417), software publishers (5112), computer systems design (5415), pharma (3254) and electronics (334), multiplied by your eligibility share.
  2. Claimants: IRS count of companies filing Form 6765.
  3. State view: each state's pool, and how many would be unclaimed if the national rate held there. No state claim counts are published.
  4. To get a real number: IRS SOI special tabulations by state and entity type, state revenue credit reports (Iowa, California and others publish claimant counts), Census business R&D surveys (BRDIS/BERD), and Form 8974 payroll-offset counts.

Blind spots founders miss

Rules that quietly shrink or delay the money.

University partnershipsPayments to qualified universities for basic research can earn a separate credit under the regular method.
Grant-funded work doesn't countSBIR or customer-funded research can fall under the funded-research exclusion. Grants and credits don't always stack.
Payroll-offset timingElected on the income tax return, then used via Form 8974 with quarterly payroll filings. Wrong order delays the cash.
Section 174A and 280CThe 2025 tax law (OBBBA) restored immediate expensing of US R&D costs from 2025. The small-business window to apply it back to 2022–24 closed July 6, 2026. You now choose between the full credit with a smaller deduction, or the reduced credit.
State conformity gapsA federal claim doesn't mean a state claim. Many state credits are non-refundable, capped or lottery-based. California gives startups with no R&D history 1.3%, and Texas caps its credit at half the franchise tax owed.
Spinout data is thinNo consistent public data on university spinouts by state exists, so we don't score it. It's a real gap.
Passthrough entitiesMany startups are LLCs or S-corps, which public IRS sector tables exclude, hiding true claim patterns.
Offshore R&DResearch performed outside the US isn't qualified. Distributed teams can quietly shrink the base.

Build the record before anyone asks for it

Since 2022 the IRS requires refund claims to identify each business component, the research activities, who did them, what they sought to discover, and the expenses. From tax year 2026, Form 6765 Section G asks for this project by project. Small businesses taking the payroll offset, and original returns with QREs of $1.5M or less and receipts of $50M or less, may skip Section G, but these records are still your audit defense.     Data & limits

    • IRS SOI Form 6765: sector totals from Publication 16 Table 14, tax years 2015 and 2022 (latest), and claims by size of receipts (2013, the latest size breakdown). C-corps only.
    • Estimator rules: federal ASC at 6% (no prior R&D) or 14% above half the 3-year average, full credit unless you tick the 280C election; payroll offset capped at $250K against Social Security (6.2% up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500) plus $250K against Medicare (1.45%); usable from the first quarter after the income tax return is filed. California ASC is 1.3% with no prior R&D (3% otherwise); Texas is 4.361% (8.722% with history) and capped at half the franchise tax owed.
    • Census County Business Patterns 2022: establishments in five R&D-intensive industries as the eligible pool. Establishments are not firms, and industry is not eligibility.
    • Claim-rate model and Opportunity index: our own models with stated assumptions, not measured rates. No public data reports claimed-versus-eligible by state.
    • USAspending.gov API: SBIR/STTR keyword proxy (live by state, with cached fallback) and NSF + NIH awards to universities (snapshot), state and county. Not official SBIR or HERD totals.
    • Grants.gov Search API: browsers are blocked by CORS, so the site shows a dated snapshot pulled from the live API. The badge says which you see.
    • State credit table: hand-compiled headline rates for 21 states; no public API exists. Rules change yearly.
    • Correlations: computed across 50 states. Correlation is not causation.
    • Hurdles list: practitioner-reported, not survey-measured.
    • Privacy: calculator inputs never leave your browser. Audit-trail entries stay in this browser until you export them, and "Delete my data" erases them. The only network calls are public-data reads (USAspending, Grants.gov, map files) and they carry none of your inputs. No accounts, cookies or analytics.
